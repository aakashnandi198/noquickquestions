The Universal Scale of Question Duration

Where does your inquiry actually land on the reality spectrum?

30s Acceptable
“Where is the printer paper?”
Legitimate quick question. A direct, factual pointer.
5 min Borderline
“Do we still support Safari 14?”
Could be quick, unless it triggers a 40-minute browser matrix debate.
45 min Liar
“Why is staging throwing 500s on checkout?”
Requires repo inspection, log parsing, and active debugging.
3 weeks Straight to Jail
“How should our distributed data model scale?”
That’s not a question; that’s an entire quarter’s OKR.

The 3 Unspoken Laws of the “Quick Question”

Why this conversational habit creates so much friction:

⚖️

1. The Asymmetry of Effort

Asking “Why doesn't the button work?” takes 4 seconds to type. Answering it requires checking out an old branch, sacrificing a goat to Webpack, reading 8 pages of StackOverflow, and re-evaluating your life choices.

🦹

2. Conversational Hostage-Taking

If someone asks: “Can you spare 2 hours to debug my mess?”, you can politely decline. Asking for a “quick question” is emotional blackmail: declining makes you look like an office villain who personally hates teamwork.

📦

3. Schrödinger’s Question

A question is simultaneously 30 seconds and 4 hours long until you reply “What's up?”. Once observed, it almost invariably collapses into a full-scale incident response call.

How to Ask Like a Civilized Human Being

Follow this 4-step formula and become everyone's favorite teammate:

01

Skip the preamble

Don't ask if you can ask. Don't say hello and wait. Just ask the question in your very first message.

02

Provide context upfront

Include links, error traces, screenshots, or PR numbers so the recipient can diagnose without back-and-forth.

03

Clarify urgency

State clearly whether this is an active production fire or something to check out whenever they have downtime.

04

Give an exit ramp

Allow them to respond asynchronously: “No rush at all, ping me whenever you're out of flow!”

🌟 The Golden Rule
“Do not label your question as 'quick.' Ask the question, provide the context, and let the person doing the work decide how long it takes.”
